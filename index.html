    A little structure. A better practice.

    1. Pick progressions and keys. Choose one or more progressions, the keys to play them in, how long each one lasts, and how many cycles to play.
    2. Play it on a loop. The chords are shown in order. Strum through them, then go back to the first chord and repeat until the timer runs out. Shapes are shown below the chords: numbers are fingers, ○ is an open string, × means don’t play it, and strings run from low E on the left to high e on the right.
    3. Follow the flow. When the timer ends, the next progression comes up in a new key. Each cycle plays every progression once, in a random order.

    Why keep the order?

    The order is what makes a progression. Each chord pulls toward the next, and V going home to I is the sound your ear expects. Shuffled, the same four chords don’t sound like the song any more. Leave As written on for real practice. Use Shuffled as a drill when you want the changes themselves to become automatic, whichever chord comes next. To vary practice and keep it musical, choose more keys instead.

    Roman numerals name each chord by its place in the key: uppercase is major, lowercase is minor, and ♭VII is the chord a semitone below the seventh note of the scale. Pause anytime with the space bar.
